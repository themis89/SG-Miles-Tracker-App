Lookalike Trap Breaker

90% of students who still struggle after 2 years get tricked by these identical shapes. Here is the anatomical rule for each:

1. The Katakana Quad (シ vs ツ vs ソ vs ン)

Distinguish by the angle and origin stroke direction!

シ
[shi] - Horizontal eyes Dashes lie horizontally. The big stroke sweeps from bottom-left up to the right (looking up).
ツ
[tsu] - Vertical drops Dashes stand upright. The big stroke slashes top-right down to the left (droplets falling).
ン
[n] - Sweeping up Single flat dash. The long stroke rises gently from the bottom upward.
ソ
[so] - Slashing down Single vertical dash. The long stroke chops from the top downwards.

2. Directional Loops (さ vs ち)

Hiragana mirrored orientation.

さ
[sa] - Loop to the RIGHT Curved belly opens towards the right hand side.
ち
[chi] - Loop to the LEFT Looks like a number "5". Main belly sweeps to the left.

3. Tail Loops (ぬ vs め) & (る vs ろ)

Check whether the ending stroke closes into a ruby/noodle loop!

ぬ
[nu] - Has a loop tail Twirled chopsticks holding a noodle loop at the very tip.
め
[me] - No loop tail Smooth tail slash. You dropped the noodle!
る
[ru] - Ruby loop Curled loop at the bottom holding a ruby.
ろ
[ro] - Open mouth Empty bottom. A robber stole the ruby!

4. The Triple Threat (ね vs れ vs わ)

Identical left backbone. Focus exclusively on the right foot.

ね
[ne] - Cat tail loop Tied in a knot or curled like a sleepy cat's tail (Neko).
れ
[re] - Sharp kick Kicks sharply out to the right like a resting soccer player.
わ
[wa] - Smooth inward curve Round wasp belly curling inward without loops.
